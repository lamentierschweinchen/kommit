# Kommit — writer signatures

Project-level profile for the `/copyeditor` skill (`~/.claude/skills/copyeditor`). The skill ships a built-in Claude-family profile; this file records patterns caught specifically in Kommit's own copy, so every future audit starts by hunting them. Per the skill: record the pattern, a verbatim caught example, and the fix that was accepted. Add to it after each review.

Most Kommit long-form copy (manifesto, `/conviction-markets`, pitch scripts) was drafted by Claude with Lukas editing, so the built-in Claude-family profile applies too. The entries below are the Kommit-specific forms those patterns took.

---

## 1. Every section ends on a crafted closer

**First caught:** 2026-10-03, `/copyeditor` corpus pass on `/manifesto`.

**Pattern:** each section of a long-form piece closes on a balanced couplet, fragment triple, or aphoristic kicker. Every individual closer reads as earned; lined up, they become a metronome by the fourth section. A form of built-in signatures #1 (resolved couplets) and #12 (uniform cleverness, no rest lines). Invisible to the writer from inside a single section; only shows when the closers are read in sequence.

**Caught examples (manifesto, before the fix), 8 sections, 8 crafted closers:**
- "The platform doesn't have to charge a fee. The substrate already pays it."
- "The unit is bigger than the platform. The platform is bigger than us."
- "A system that treats them the same is a system that hasn't priced patience."
- "One unit. Three groups. The same evidence."
- "The misreadings are familiar. The unit isn't."
- "The primitive is the point."
- "Patience should be measurable. Conviction should be portable. None of the three groups should pay for the privilege of caring early."
- "The architecture exists. Build it with us, build it differently, build it better. Just build it."

**Fix accepted:** keep the strongest two or three (here: "bigger than us," the patience/conviction line, "Just build it"); end the other sections on their last plain fact. Thinning, not elimination.

**Hunt:** list the last sentence of every section side by side before reviewing anything else.

## 2. Em-dash over-correction

**First caught:** 2026-10-03, same pass.

**Pattern:** after an "avoid em dashes" instruction, the manifesto went to zero dashes while sibling page `/conviction-markets` kept using them freely. Two pages on one site, two punctuation registers. The zero-dash page also read clipped: some asides want a dash, and parentheses or full stops are worse there.

**Fix accepted:** a middle register. A dash for a genuine aside or one point of emphasis (e.g. "dismissed in 2023 — on standing, not on the merits"); never as a rhythm device. Judge dash use across sibling pages, not one page at a time.

## 3. Deliberate repetitions (protect, do not flag)

- **"Three groups"** (backers, founders, investors) recurs across the manifesto and `/conviction-markets`. It is the thesis spine.
- **"It is not a launchpad / a token / equity crowdfunding / a casino"** in the manifesto's "What this is not" section. A deliberate list of real misreadings.
- **"We are not the only possible builder"** heading. The script's unscoped-"only" grep hits it; it is a disclaimer, not a superlative.

## Kommit voice signals to protect

From the same review: plain declaratives ("Three groups want the same thing."), native crypto detail used sparingly ("bought your bag"), concrete specimens (the Anna worked example, "CEO" to "co-CEO"), and the candid sections ("Where it can fail"), which are the least-worked and most human prose in the corpus. Pull other copy toward that register.
